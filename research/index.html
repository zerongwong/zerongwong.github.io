Current project · Survey project in progress

The Economics of Consumption Stimulus: Household Spending, Market Incidence, and Welfare

This project asks what one dollar of public spending intended to stimulate consumption ultimately buys. It distinguishes three empirical objects that are often conflated: net additional household expenditure; market incidence through prices, quantities, profits, labor, and capacity; and welfare after fiscal costs, externalities, stabilization, and distribution.

The review compares cash payments, rebates, vouchers, temporary consumption-tax cuts, and purchase subsidies using a common estimand map. Its central argument is that no instrument-invariant stimulus multiplier exists: similar observed spending responses can imply different real and welfare effects because liquidity, conditionality, market structure, supply elasticity, and macroeconomic slack govern the passage between the three ledgers.

Policy instruments

  • Cash payments and temporary rebates
  • Digital and paper consumption vouchers
  • Temporary VAT and sales-tax reductions
  • Purchase and replacement subsidies

Key moderators

  • Liquidity and policy conditionality
  • Market power and supply elasticity
  • Tradability and capacity constraints
  • Macroeconomic slack and inflation
