About

Household behavior, markets, and policy

My current work examines consumption-stimulus policies, with particular attention to the distinction between household spending responses, market incidence, and social welfare. I use household survey data, administrative records, and applied microeconometric methods.

My published work includes studies of digital finance, household leverage, stock-market participation, asset allocation, and consumption in China. A broader strand applies microdata to questions in education, migration, regional development, and public policy.

Current research

The Economics of Consumption Stimulus: Household Spending, Market Incidence, and Welfare

Survey project in progress

What does one dollar of public spending intended to stimulate consumption ultimately buy?

The project distinguishes net additional household expenditure, market incidence through prices and supply responses, and welfare after fiscal, distributional, and externality considerations.
